Quantum Computing Fundamentals: Qubits, Superposition, and Entanglement

By Yash Khebade | Published on October 1, 2026 | 10 min read

Introduction to Quantum Computing

Quantum computing represents a paradigm shift in how we process information. Unlike classical bits that exist strictly in a state of 0 or 1, quantum bits (qubits) leverage the principles of quantum mechanics to exist in multiple states simultaneously. This fundamentally alters computational complexity for specific classes of algorithms.

Superposition: The Power of Probability

In a classical digital circuit, a wire carries either a high voltage (VDD) or a low voltage (GND). A qubit, however, can be in a linear combination of states |psi> = alpha|0> + beta|1>, where alpha and beta are probability amplitudes. This property allows a quantum processor to evaluate exponentially large state spaces in parallel.

Quantum Entanglement

Entanglement is perhaps the most counterintuitive property. When two qubits become entangled, the state of one instantaneously determines the state of the other, regardless of spatial separation. In quantum algorithms like Shor's and Grover's, entanglement is used to probabilistically amplify the correct answers while canceling out incorrect ones via interference.

Simulating Logic Gates

While SQGATE focuses on classical digital CMOS logic, understanding quantum gates (like the Hadamard, CNOT, and Pauli-X) provides a fascinating contrast to traditional AND/OR logic. A classical NOT gate simply inverts; a quantum Pauli-X gate rotates the qubit state 180 degrees around the X-axis of the Bloch sphere.

from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
qc = QuantumCircuit(2)
# Apply Hadamard to Qubit 0
qc.h(0)
# Apply CNOT with Control=0, Target=1
qc.cx(0, 1)
print(qc.draw())
